Biography

Thomas Hervé Mboa Nkoudou is an Assistant Professor of Organizational Communication in the Department of Communication, Faculty of Arts, and a researcher at the Centre for Law, Technology and Society at the University of Ottawa. His research lies at the intersection of organizational communication, responsible artificial intelligence, AI governance, and emerging agentic systems. Drawing on critical and decolonial perspectives, his work examines how AI systems are developed, introduced, governed, and scaled across different organizational and societal contexts, with particular attention to inclusion, responsibility, and the common good.

His current research program, Critical Scaling, examines what happens after an AI system becomes technically capable: how it is introduced into organizations, how it reshapes work and decision-making, how responsibilities are redistributed, and what conditions are required for its responsible adoption and expansion. The program seeks to understand how AI systems can move from technical capability to broader organizational and societal use while remaining responsible, trustworthy, and responsive to the contexts in which they operate.

Before joining the University of Ottawa, he was a Researcher-in-Residence at the Centre d’expertise international de Montréal en intelligence artificielle (CEIMIA), where his work focused on developing approaches to strengthen the inclusion and representation of African perspectives within the global AI ecosystem. As an emerging researcher with the Open African Innovation Research (Open AIR) Network, he was also a visiting researcher at the University of Ottawa’s Centre for Law, Technology and Society through the Queen Elizabeth II Diamond Jubilee Scholarships program.

He holds a PhD in Public Communication from Université Laval. His research and publications address issues including responsible AI, open science, cognitive justice, inclusive innovation, and the governance of emerging technologies. He has also contributed to international discussions on these issues through lectures, research collaborations, and engagements with international organizations.
