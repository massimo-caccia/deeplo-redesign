Julian Huppert

Director of the Intellectual Forum, Jesus College, Cambridge
Jesus College in the University of Cambridge · United Kingdom
🌐 https://www.jesus.cam.ac.uk/people/julian-huppert

Biography

Julian Huppert is an academic and politician, and has been Founding Director of the Intellectual Forum at Jesus College, Cambridge, since 2016.

He completed a PhD in Biological Chemistry in 2005 studying unusual structures of DNA. In particular, DNA of particular sequences can form four-stranded knot-like structures called G-quadruplexes, which can function as genomic switches, turning genes on and off. These are important as anti-cancer drug targets. He secured a Junior Research Fellowship at Trinity College, Cambridge, was a Research Councils UK Academic Fellow, and held a University Lectureship in the Cavendish Laboratory, as part of the Physics of Medicine initiative.

In parallel to that, he was involved in politics, serving as a County Councillor and Leader of the Opposition on Cambridgeshire County Council, and then as Member of Parliament for Cambridge 2010-5. He was the Lib Dem Spokesperson on Home Affairs, Justice and Equalities, served on the Home Affairs Select Committee and Joint Committee on Human Rights, and among other achievements was heavily involved in the passage of the Marriage (Same Sex Couples) Act 2013. As an MP, he was particularly noted for his work on civil liberties, science, cycling, and technology policy, winning awards ranging from being named ISPA ‘Internet Hero of the Year’ to Parliamentary Beard of the Year, both in 2013.

He has held a number of roles in the NHS, including serving as Vice Chair and Audit Chair of the NHS Cambridgeshire and Peterborough Clinical Commissioning Group, overseeing an annual budget of £1.8 billion. He is Chair of the Ethics Committee for NHS Central East, and is a Non-Executive Director of Health Innovation East. He is also a Council Member of the Nuffield Council on Bioethics.

He is a Director of the Joseph Rowntree Reform Trust Ltd, and a Trustee of JRSST-CT. He was Chair of the UK Democracy Fund 2019-25, raising ~£5 million to help build a healthy democracy, in which everyone can participate and where political power is shared fairly. Highlights include getting over 750,000 additional people registered to vote in 2024.

He has worked with a range of technology companies about ethics and governance, including being Founding Chair of the Panel of Independent Reviewers for DeepMind Health. He was Chair of the AI Ethics Working Group for the Home Office’ Biometrics and Forensics Ethics Group, a Ministerial appointment. He was convenor for the Edtech Evidence Group, and an advisor for Sparx Learning.

He is a Visiting Professor at the Policy Institute, King’s College London, and a Fellow of the Royal Society of New South Wales, the oldest learned society in the Southern Hemisphere. He has recently completed a law degree, ‘for fun’.
